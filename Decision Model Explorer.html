Which benchmark says what

The independent boards rank models differently, so this page never mixes them in one chart. Pick the board closest to your task.

How to read a Capability score

JevBench, ImageJevBench and OpenDecisions report Capability on 0–100: the mean of Intelligence (chance-corrected accuracy) and Calibration (do the probabilities mean what they say). A model only counts as “Jev-class” on Benchmark Heaven's boards if it stays within 2× Jev's cost and latency.

Capability = (Intelligence + Calibration) / 2
Chon-1.0-12B: (79.9 + 90.8) / 2 = 85.3

Calibration is cheap to improve: temperature scaling alone took zero-shot Gemma 4 31B from 72.9 to 79.6.

How the “Fits my GPU” estimate works

VRAM ≈ parameters × bytes per weight × 1.2
bytes: BF16 = 2 · FP8 = 1 · 4-bit ≈ 0.55 (incl. scales)

The extra 20% covers KV cache and activations for short decision prompts. Check: d3 (26.1B) at BF16 gives ≈63 GB, and vLLM-SR measured a 62–65 GiB peak per replica. MoE models (26B-A4B) still load every expert, so total size counts. Closed models and undisclosed sizes are hidden when this filter is on.

Where Jev still wins

  • Knowledge-heavy multiple choice (BoolQ, MMLU, MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge, WinoGrande, SciQ): Jev averages 92.9 against 88.8 for the trained Gemma 4 31B in the Harvard / Kempner paper.
  • Option-order stability: the paper's trained 4B model still changes its answer on about 6% of questions when the option order is reversed.
  • Traffic: Jev 1.13 and 1.14 together carry over a billion OpenRouter requests a week.
